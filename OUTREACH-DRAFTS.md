measured learning outcomes yet. **Not one line below claims any of those.**
No invented student quotes, no "join 10,000 learners", no "proven to
improve fluency 3x". Everything below sells only what the product verifiably
does today. If a piece feels less punchy than typical startup copy, that's
why — and it's the right trade until you have real numbers.

**19.** Workplace English isn't the big presentation. It's the quick update
in a meeting, the follow-up you'd rather say out loud first, the small talk
before things start. That's what most "business English" training misses —
and what LEXIS is built for. learnwithlexis.com/practice/business-english

**20.** A technical note, for anyone building voice AI:
Most voice assistants route audio through their own server. LEXIS connects
your browser directly to the model over WebRTC — the server only mints a
short-lived credential, then gets out of the way. That one architectural
choice is why there's no lag between you stopping and her replying.

**21.** We just moved LEXIS onto OpenAI's newer Realtime model. ~25% lower
p95 latency, per OpenAI's published figures. For a product whose entire
pitch is "no awkward pauses," latency isn't a metric — it's the product.

**22.** Hiring managers in Thailand: candidates who can do the job often
interview badly in English, and you lose good people to a language gap that
has nothing to do with competence. Worth knowing tools exist for that now.

**23.** Bilingual voice AI is harder than it looks. Most models either
default back to one language or go silent when a sentence mixes two. Half
of every LEXIS conversation is deliberately in the language the student
hasn't mastered — so code-switching mid-sentence had to actually work.

**24.** Building in public: LEXIS is a voice-native English/Thai tutor.

# Before you send anything

1. **Every link should be trackable.** Add UTM parameters so you can tell
   which of these actually worked. Untracked outreach teaches you nothing.
2. **Cold email has legal rules.** Thailand's PDPA and (for any EU/UK
   recipients) GDPR both apply to unsolicited commercial email. Business
   contact addresses are generally lower-risk, but check before bulk-sending.
3. **Do not mass-send sections 5 and 6 unmodified.** They're structures, not
   scripts. One real personalised line per recipient changes the response
   rate more than any copy tweak.
4. **Start narrow.** Pick your two best channels and do them properly rather
   than firing all nine at once — with no data yet, spreading thin means you
   learn nothing from any of them.
5. **Post 21 has a dependency**: it cites OpenAI's own published latency
   figure for the model upgrade. That's accurate as a citation of their
   claim, but LEXIS has not independently measured it. Keep the attribution
   in if you post it.
